# A corpus with no author

Generated by `real_corpus.py`. Do not edit by hand.

Every other question set in this repository was written by somebody - by the analyst, by a
different model, by hand to a specification. This one has no author: the records are the real
package.json files installed under `node_modules`, and the questions are derived
from their descriptions by a rule that is applied without judgement.

| band | how the question was made |
|---|---|
| easy | the description, verbatim |
| hard | the same description with every third long word removed |
| adversarial | the description of a record that shares three or more long words with another |
| absent | the description of a real package that was left out of the store |

## The validator rejects this corpus, and it is right

`validate_corpus.py` was written for the authored corpora and it fails this one, on
the hard band: **16 of 16** hard questions still share more than half their long words with the
record that answers them (median share 1.00). The rule that made them removed every third word,
and a sentence with a third of its words cut out is still a sentence a substring counter can
answer.

The same file's baseline pins all sixteen as accepted, because a check that always fails is a
check nobody reads - and a pinned defect that stops happening is reported as fixed, so the pin
is a statement about this corpus rather than an exemption from the rule.

One thing the generator does enforce is the absent band's own definition, because that one is
mechanical: a question counts as unanswerable only if no stored record covers more than half of
its long words. The first build broke that rule four times and the validator said so.

That is the limit of an authorless corpus, stated precisely: real records are free, real
questions are nearly free, and **a paraphrased band needs somebody to paraphrase**. What this
corpus buys is a store and a question set nobody chose, and what it cannot buy is the band that
separates a keyword matcher from a semantic one. The easy, adversarial and absent bands are real
tests; the hard band here is a keyword band wearing the name.

## What this does not settle

It is real text and it is not real memory. These are package descriptions: short, written to sell
a library, and sharing vocabulary because the vocabulary is a domain's rather than a person's. A
question a colleague asks a memory store is longer, more ambiguous and often answerable only from
several records at once. What the corpus does buy is that no sentence in it was written by anyone
who knew what the answer would be, which is the property every other corpus here lacks.
