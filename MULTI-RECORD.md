## What the single-answer metric cannot see

A question whose answer is a set of three records scored by `hit3` is scored by whether ONE of
them came back - the arbitrary first member of the cluster. That is why the last two columns are
not the same number, and the gap between them is the measurement this phase exists for.

**2 of 11 subjects answer every cluster in full and 6 answer none of them.** The 2 are `control 1.0.0`, `zenbrain 0.3.0`;
the 6 are `mcp-duckdb-memory-server`, `mcp-knowledge-graph`, `memory-lean`, `server-memory`, `server-memory`, `statecore-mcp`.

5 of those 6 answer every single-record question on the same store, so the difference
between them is not retrieval quality. It is whether returning a set is something the
subject does at all: a search that answers with one id and stops cannot answer a question
whose answer is three.

And where a subject does return part of a set, the single-record metric counts the cluster as
answered: `memento 0.7.0` is scored 12 of 16 that way, against 9 answered in full. The overcount is the
size of the difference between answering a question and answering part of it.

So the honest reading of this table is not a ranking. It is that a question with three answers
tests something a question with one answer does not, that a minority of this table does it, and
that every metric this repository has published would have described the others as competent
retrievers on the strength of the single-record column.

## What this does not settle

The search is asked for three results, so a cluster of three can be answered only by a subject that
returns three relevant records rather than three records. Raising k would make the question
answerable by every subject that finds one member, which measures nothing; keeping k at three
makes it answerable only by a subject that ranks the whole cluster, which is the harder and more
honest question but is also a question about k.

It also does not test a join. Reading one record to discover the names of others - what a real
multi-hop question needs - is a different capability, and no subject here claims it.

## One thing the store had to be shaped around

Every record text is capped at 300 characters because `memory-lean` refuses to store more: it
answers a create with "observations over 300 chars or not strings" and the run does not start. That
is a documented limit of the subject and not a defect, but it is worth saying that a corpus the
size of a real store would have to be written to fit it, and that one subject in this table cannot
hold a record longer than a long sentence.
