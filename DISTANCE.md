# How much paraphrase does a memory survive

Generated by `distance_sweep.py`. Do not edit by hand.

The four corpora disagree, and the disagreement is one axis: how far the question sits from the text
that answers it. The authored corpora paraphrase; the real one asks with the record's own words. This
walks that distance in five steps, from a verbatim question to one that keeps two words of the answer,
using real package descriptions and a rule rather than a writer.

| band | the question keeps | of the record's long words, measured |
|---|---|---|
| d0 | every word | 1.00 |
| d1 | two of every three | 0.71 |
| d2 | every second | 0.56 |
| d3 | one in three | 0.33 |
| d4 | the first two, in a question | 0.33 |

The ladder is measured rather than assumed: the last two steps are close together because a
description with seven long words leaves two of them to a question, which is a third of the record
and not a fifth. The curve is read with that compression in mind.

## Where the four corpora sit on this axis

ONE NUMBER IS NOT ENOUGH TO PLACE A QUESTION, and this table needs two. How much of the
answering record survives into the question is the distance the sweep walks. How much of the
question is actually drawn from the record is a second coordinate, and the ladder cannot move it:
a question built by deleting words from a record uses only that record's words, so its second
coordinate is always 1.00.

| corpus | of the record, in the question | of the question, in the record | answerable questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.29 | 0.50 | 48 |
| B | 0.33 | 0.57 | 48 |
| C | 0.22 | 0.60 | 47 |
| real | 0.71 | 1.00 | 48 |

The authored corpora sit low on both coordinates and the real one sits high on the first and at
1.00 on the second. That is the whole of the disagreement between them, and it also says the
ladder below is the easy half of the axis: every step of it is still a question made from the
record's own words, which is a thing no person has ever asked.

## Where the ranking stops being the same ranking

Every pair of subjects, compared on the same questions in each band: how many pairs this sweep
separates at the nearest distance, and how many at the furthest.

- **d0**: 26 of 55 pairs separated, on 24 questions each
- **d1**: 35 of 55 pairs separated, on 24 questions each
- **d2**: 38 of 55 pairs separated, on 24 questions each
- **d3**: 34 of 55 pairs separated, on 24 questions each
- **d4**: 33 of 55 pairs separated, on 16 questions each

The nearest distance separates fewer pairs than the middle one, because a question that quotes the
record is answered by everybody with a substring counter and the top of the table becomes a tie at
the ceiling. A corpus placed at the near end is not a stricter instrument; it is one with no room
at the top, which is exactly what the real corpus turned into.

## What this does not settle

A distance sweep is not a paraphrase. Removing words is not the same operation as saying the same
thing differently, and a subject that reads words will look worse here than one that reads meaning
only in the sense that its own words are being taken away. What the sweep does buy is a number per
subject instead of a contradiction between corpora: each of the four question sets is one point on
these curves, and the disagreement between them is the slope between those points.
