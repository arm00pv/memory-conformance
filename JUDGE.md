## Do the rule and the reader agree

Treating the rule's reading as a labeller - answer or near means relevant, noise means not - the
two agree on **45 of 54 pairs (83%)**, and Cohen's kappa is **0.67**.

Kappa is reported beside the raw agreement because the two classes are not equally frequent: a
labeller that called everything relevant would agree with the rule on every answer and every near
miss and be wrong about all of the noise. Kappa subtracts exactly that, and above 0.6 is usually
called substantial, 0.4 to 0.6 moderate.

**Every disagreement in this sample runs one way, and that is the result.** The rule calls
9 records near misses that the reader calls unrelated, and calls nothing noise that the
reader would use. So the three-way split is wrong in the direction the report already claims
for it: the noise count is a **floor**, because a record this reading excuses as a near miss
may be junk a caller pays for. That sentence was a caveat in PRECISION.md; it is a measurement
now, and it says the published noise figures are optimistic by at least 9 of 34 near-miss
credits in this sample.

## What this does not settle

One model at one temperature is not a standard. It is a reader, and readers disagree with each
other; the number here is how far a particular reader is from a particular rule, not how far the
rule is from the truth. A second model would make that visible, and a third would make it
quantifiable, and neither is done.

The prompt is a judgement call of its own. It asks whether the record would help, which is the
question the report claims not to be able to answer; it does not ask whether it is the answer,
because that is what the corpus already says and asking it would test nothing.
