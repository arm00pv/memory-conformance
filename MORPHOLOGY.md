# Does a memory survive a plural

Generated by `morph_sweep.py`. Do not edit by hand.

The distance ladder moves one coordinate: how much of a record survives into a question. Its other
coordinate is fixed at 1.00, because a question built by deleting words uses only the record's own
words - and a person does not ask that way. This moves the other one. Every description is asked
four times with a growing share of its long words pluralised, so the question contains words the
record does not:

| band | the question | vocabulary the record lacks, measured |

| m1 | every fourth long word pluralised | 0.14 |
| m2 | every second long word pluralised | 0.38 |
| m3 | every long word pluralised | 0.83 |

## The two coordinates together

A question can lose the record's words in two independent ways: by asking about less of it, and by
asking in vocabulary it does not contain. Both are measured now, on the same records.

| sweep | of the record, in the question | of the question, in the record |
|---|---|---|
| distance, d0 to d4 | 1.00 down to 0.29 | **1.00 at every band** |
| morphology, m0 to m3 | **1.00 at every band** | 1.00 down to 0.17 |

Neither sweep alone is what a person writes. A real question changes both at once, and the two
failure modes they isolate are different ones: losing the record's words is a recall problem, and
using its own words is a normalisation problem.

## What this does not settle

Pluralisation is one morphological operation and the easiest one there is: the stem is intact and
two letters have changed. A subject that fails here fails the cheapest case of new vocabulary, which
makes this a floor rather than a measure of understanding - the same role the hard band of the real
corpus plays. What it does establish is that the second coordinate can be moved at all without a
writer, a thesaurus or a network connection.
